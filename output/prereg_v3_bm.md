# prereg_v3_bm: book-to-market replication-lite (separate from the v3 headline)

Written BEFORE any return in this analysis is computed. Nothing below was chosen from returns. Script: `30_v3_bm_replication.py`; output `output/spec_v3/bm_replication_v3.csv`.

## What this is, and what it is not
This is NOT a replication of Piotroski (2000). He applied the score to the highest book-to-market quintile of the whole Compustat universe, which is dominated by small, illiquid value firms; he found the effect concentrated there. Our universe is the 500 largest US non-financial non-utility firms, so it cannot contain small value firms. The closest test available without a new data pull is the highest book-to-market quintile INSIDE the universe. A real replication needs a broad-universe WRDS pull that the project owner has not authorised. This test is labelled "replication-lite", is outside the primary families of prereg_v3, and its result must not be read as confirming or refuting the paper.

## Decisions and reasons
1. **Sample.** The included names of each formation date (`universe.included`), scored by the v2 scores under the LITERAL issuance rule (the v3 primary rule), aligned by `12b_spec_v2_scores.align` (filing date + 3 days, at most MAX_SCORE_AGE_MONTHS old). Reason: identical to the v3 headline mechanics, so only the value cut is new.
2. **Fiscal year used for book-to-market.** The same fiscal year that supplies the name's score on that date (same availability rule: filing date + 3 days). Reason: no look-ahead, and score and value ratio describe the same balance sheet.
3. **Book equity** = `ceq` (Compustat common equity, total), no deferred-tax add-back. Reason: Piotroski uses book value of equity at fiscal year-end; the plain reported figure is the least manipulated, conservative choice. (The v1 value_filter_B used ceq + txditc with formation-date CRSP market cap and terciles; this differs on purpose, so the two are not identical tests.)
4. **Market value** = `prcc_f * csho` at the fiscal year-end of that same year. Reason: Piotroski measures market value at the end of the fiscal year before formation, matching the book value date; it is also known at formation. Limitation: `prcc_f*csho` covers one share class only, so multi-class firms get a too-small market value and too-high book-to-market. This is stated, not corrected (no new data). Rows with `prcc_f` or `csho` missing or not positive, `ceq` missing, or `curcd` not USD (as in v1 version B) are NOT ranked and drop out of this analysis.
5. **Book-to-market** = book equity / market value. Non-positive book equity stays ranked (lowest, never in the top quintile), as in v1; it is not dropped.
6. **Quintile rule.** On each formation date, among scored, USD, rankable included names, the top quintile is every name with book-to-market >= the exact 0.8 linear-interpolation quantile (exact-fraction rule, as `05_align.exact_quantile`). Ties at the cutoff are all included (>=). Quintiles are formed over rankable scored names only, since unscored names cannot be High or Low and the paper also required the data.
7. **Buckets** inside the quintile: High = existing `cfg.HIGH_SCORES` (8,9), Low = `cfg.LOW_SCORES` (0-4), Middle remainder. Not tuned.
8. **Portfolios** (v3 mechanics, literal rule, copied unchanged from `22_v3_robustness.py`): High, Low, and Benchmark = equal weight of the WHOLE quintile (High, Middle, Low). Buy and hold 12 months, pro rata redistribution on exit, one-sided charge on reinvestment, approved hold-flat months, 10 bps on turnover, first portfolio charged as bought from cash. Each series has its own drift weights.
9. **Thin buckets.** MIN_BUCKET_NAMES = 10. Every formation date with fewer than 10 names in High, in Low, or in the quintile is counted and reported. The rule does not change. If a bucket has ZERO names on a date, that portfolio has no return for that date's 12 months; those months are dropped from every comparison involving that bucket (pairs are formed only where both series exist) and the count of dropped months is reported. The drift state of that portfolio is reset, so its next portfolio is charged as bought from cash.
10. **Tests (two, the family).**
   (a) Mean of the monthly High minus Benchmark(quintile) net return, times 12.
   (b) Alpha of the same monthly spread on four factors (Mkt-RF, SMB, HML, MOM from `ff_factors.parquet`), times 12.
   Both: OLS with Newey-West HAC errors, lag = floor(4*(T/100)^(2/9)), no small-sample correction (as in the v3 tests), p-values from t with T-k degrees of freedom (k = 1 for the mean, 5 for the alpha). Holm adjustment over these two p-values. Minimum detectable effect at 80% power, 5% two-sided: (t_{0.975,df} + t_{0.8,df}) * SE, annualised x12.
11. **Descriptive, outside the Holm family.** High minus Low inside the quintile (mean x12 and four-factor alpha, same HAC and df rules); High, Low and Benchmark CAGRs; counts of names per bucket per date. No shorting costs; diagnostic only.
12. **Reading rule.** With few names per bucket the test will be low-powered; the MDE is reported so that a null is read as "cannot rule out Piotroski's size of effect" unless the interval excludes it. Nothing is re-run with a different quintile, cutoff or definition after seeing the output.
